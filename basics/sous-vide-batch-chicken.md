## Ingredients

- 3 lb (1.36kg) boneless, skinless chicken breasts or thighs, in two 1½ lb (680g) dinner portions
- 2 tablespoons (30ml) olive oil, divided among bags
- 4 garlic cloves (12g), crushed and divided
- 2 teaspoons (2g) dried Italian herbs, without salt
- No salt in the freezer bags
- For weeknight searing: 1 tablespoon (15ml) neutral oil per dinner portion
- Optional separate fish batch: 1½ lb (680g) salmon in four portions no thicker than ¾ inch (2cm), with 1 teaspoon (5ml) olive oil

**Sodium notes:** Finishing sauce is the main source; buy unbrined chicken with no injected saline, keep bags salt-free and finish with a measured low-sodium sauce.

## Instructions

1. **Bag on arrival:** Flatten breasts to no more than ¾ inch (2cm) thick; unfold thighs to no more than 1 inch (2.5cm). Divide oil, garlic and herbs among vacuum bags. Lay pieces in a single layer with no overlap, seal, label by dinner and date, then freeze immediately. Several smaller bags may make up one dinner portion.

2. **Cook from frozen:** Fully heat the bath before adding frozen bags; keep every bag submerged with water circulating around it. For breasts at 145°F (63°C), cook 2–2½ hours (use 2½ hours for this prep batch). For thighs at 165°F (74°C), cook 2–3 hours (use 3 hours for the full thickness). These frozen schedules add roughly 50% to the thawed schedule and apply only to the thickness limits above. Do not stack frozen pieces or shorten the times; pink color alone does not indicate safety.

3. **Chill promptly:** Keep bags sealed and immediately immerse in plenty of ice water, replenishing ice. For these thin, single-layer bags, allow at least 1 hour and chill the centers to 40°F (4°C) or below before refrigerating. Do not leave warm bags to cool on the counter.

4. **Store dinner portions:** Label with cooking date. Keep intact, rapidly chilled bags up to 5 days only in a refrigerator verified below 38°F (3°C). If temperature is not verified, or the bag is opened, use within 3–4 days; freeze later dinners on prep day. Thaw cooked frozen portions overnight in the fridge.

5. **Sear on dinner night:** Open, discard bag liquid and pat dry. Heat 1 tablespoon (15ml) oil per dinner in a skillet; sear 1–2 minutes per side until golden. Slice and warm in the dinner sauce, covered if needed, until the center reaches 165°F (74°C). A quick sear alone does not reheat a cold center.

6. **Handle salmon separately:** For the optional soft adult texture, cook thin frozen salmon at 125°F (52°C) for about 45–60 minutes, roughly 50% longer than thawed, then sear briefly and serve immediately. This is not pasteurized fish and is not for the children or chilled batch storage. For the family, remove from the bag and finish in a skillet or oven until the center reaches 145°F (63°C), opaque and easily flaked. For vacuum-packed frozen fish, follow the label; remove packaging before refrigerator thawing if directed.

## Notes

**From frozen:** All bath times above start from frozen. Freeze bags flat; no counter-thawing.

**Sunday timing:** About 20–30 minutes hands-on; the bath and ice chill take several hours unattended. Two 1½ lb (680g) portions cover [teriyaki](../mains/chicken-teriyaki-bowls.md) and [alfredo](../mains/chicken-alfredo-fettuccine.md) for this household. Serve larger portions to the adults when dividing six nominal servings across two family dinners.

**Safety basis:** Thickness, full cooking time and rapid chilling matter for low-temperature poultry; see [Baldwin’s sous-vide guide](https://douglasbaldwin.com/sous-vide.html). For ordinary opened leftovers, follow [USDA’s 3–4-day storage guidance](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/freezing-and-food-safety).
